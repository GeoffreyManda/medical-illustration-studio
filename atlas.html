The medical illustration handbook

1. Begin with the question, not the style

An illustration succeeds when a reader can make the intended anatomical or scientific distinction without acquiring a false one. A visually pleasing heart with the wrong connections is not a lower-quality version of a correct figure; it is a different and potentially misleading explanation. AMI describes the profession as a combination of scientific understanding, visual communication and collaboration with subject experts. This toolkit therefore separates scientific specification, geometry, communication and final review. [ami-profession]

Write the figure's central statement as a sentence. “Show the epithelium” is an object request. “Explain how the luminal surface, intercellular junctions and basal interface are organised” is a communication objective. The latter determines the view, necessary structures, labels and omissions. For a mechanism, the corresponding sentence must identify the process and its evidence status. For a patient explanation, it must identify the understanding or decision the visual is intended to support.

The title may name the subject; the composition must answer the question. Do not make a specimen or a diagram decorative simply because the title is already informative. Choose the smallest complete scientific explanation rather than the smallest possible drawing.

2. Keep three reference functions separate

An identity reference establishes what a structure or process is called. A spatial or mechanistic reference supports where it is, what it connects to or what relation is asserted. A communication reference demonstrates an effective way to explain it. One source may serve more than one role, but a famous atlas plate is not a licence to copy its artwork, and an ontology label is not a geometric reference.

Use a source ledger with exact version, figure or section, the claim it supports, the reason it is relevant and the applicable reuse conditions. Distinguish reviewed sources from verification targets. An agent should not convert “a source exists in the bibliography” into “this arrow has been verified.” The source records included here deliberately retain incomplete-retrieval and verification-target states.

For anatomy, inspect more than one appropriate view when a single projection leaves spatial ambiguity. For a molecular structure, distinguish measured coordinates, a computational model and a symbolic cartoon. RCSB's policies also distinguish archive coordinate data from its educational artwork. [rcsb]

3. Define the biological contract

The contract specifies identity, relationships, variation and exclusions. At minimum it declares the subject system, species, life stage, anatomical view, laterality and scale policy. Where relevant, add body region, sex characteristics, developmental stage, clinical context and normal or pathological variation. These are not decorative descriptors; they can change which geometry is defensible.

List the structures that must be present, the structures deliberately omitted, the relationships that must remain true and the claims that are uncertain. The scene's relations array represents structural assertions without adding visual clutter. part_of, contains, branches_from, drains_into, lines, passes_through, innervates and positional predicates are different relations. The compiler catches unresolved entities, proper part-whole cycles and direct positional contradictions. It cannot establish whether an asserted course or attachment is true.

For a mechanism, build a claim ledger before drawing arrows. Established claims require references. Proposed and illustrative claims remain distinguishable in the graphic. The example signalling figure intentionally uses an abstract hypothetical mechanism rather than inventing evidence for a named receptor pathway.

4. Select the representation

A regional anatomical plate explains three-dimensional relationships. A tissue section explains organisation at a cut surface. An unfolded map explains continuity at the expense of spatial shape. A mechanism explains interactions and state changes. A structure-based molecular view explains coordinates or surfaces. A workflow explains activities and information movement. A graphical abstract explains the supported contribution of a study. These are not interchangeable styles.

Choose the representation that makes the important relationship legible with the fewest necessary transformations. A complicated organ may need a locator plus a registered section. A simple membrane interaction rarely benefits from a full-body silhouette. A long tubular system may be clearer when unfolded, provided the alteration of spatial form is explicit. A magnified inset must identify its source region and must not resemble a causal arrow.

The 28 layout recipes are organised by reader question, reading order, required inputs and invariants. Their normalized slots execute as placement scaffolds. They do not supply the anatomical geometry or decide scientific truth. The eight specimens demonstrate complete uses of selected recipes.

5. Build geometry in the right order

Start with the outer extent and principal axis. Place the major compartments and structures. Resolve adjacency, continuity, entry and exit points, branching, cut planes and hidden surfaces. Only then add internal features, tissue texture and labels. Fixing a mistaken topology after painting is slower and more error-prone than detecting it in a sparse structural drawing.

Treat meaningful negative space as part of the anatomy. A lumen is not just a blank background. A tissue plane is not a convenient gap for a label. A vessel passing behind another structure must remain distinguishable from one entering it. A line crossing is not a junction unless the figure intentionally encodes one.

Reference-based geometry can come from a reviewed vector, a correctly registered 3D model or original drawing by an illustrator. The imported asset must retain its identity, viewpoint, rights and limitations. Do not stretch an organ to occupy a layout box. The compositor preserves aspect ratio and transforms its landmarks together with the geometry.

6. Use semantic detail instead of decorative density

Add a feature because the reader needs it to recognise, distinguish or understand the object. A selected membrane protein can be drawn at a larger illustrative scale to explain its role, but the scaling must not pretend to be measured. An organelle count chosen to balance a composition is not abundance data. Colour changes and surface marks cannot be used to imply a diagnostic difference without support.

Scale and omission are deliberate editorial decisions. Goodsell's molecular landscapes provide a useful research example: their explanatory construction combines several evidence types and deliberately leaves some components out. The lesson is explicit reconstruction and selective inclusion, not a request to imitate an artist's surface treatment. [goodsell]

A restrained schematic can be excellent. A detailed anatomical rendering can also be excellent. Flatness, realism, softness, minimalism and visual complexity are not quality scores on their own. Their value depends on the scientific task and the reader.

7. Compose the reading path

Place the dominant explanatory structure first. Give it sufficient space. Establish a small number of subordinate elements: a locator, inset, counterpart or legend only when needed. Use proximity to indicate grouping, alignment to support comparison and whitespace to distinguish unrelated content. Avoid boxing every object into an interface card.

For a paired comparison, lock the viewpoint, magnification, object identity, baseline colours and label vocabulary. Change only the declared variable or state. Otherwise the viewer cannot know whether the difference reflects biology or drawing choices. The paired epithelial specimen changes only the annotation, making the invariant geometry visible without fabricating a treatment effect.

Reserve label space before the final detailed drawing. Avoid letting the figure occupy every available point and then attaching a ring of tiny labels. Reading at the intended physical size is more informative than judging the artwork at a large zoom. Audience, message and medium should shape figure design. [figure-design]

8. Label the structure, not the bounding box

A label needs an actual target. An automatic line to an asset's nearest rectangle edge is not a guarantee that it touches the named structure. Each asset therefore has normalized landmarks. Custom anatomical assets should replace generic bounding-box landmarks with specific named points or clearly identified regions.

Use short unambiguous labels, consistent naming and a controlled abbreviation policy. Keep endpoints visibly attached to the intended structure. Do not run leaders through unrelated labels. Route leader lines separately from biological connectors and use no causal arrowheads on annotations. A dot endpoint can identify the selected point without asserting directional flow.

The software checks label-box overlap, canvas bounds, available text metrics and final physical type size. It does not automatically solve every line crossing or determine whether a point is on the correct anatomical surface. Those are image-review tasks.

9. Make uncertainty visible

Separate three questions: whether the object exists, whether the depicted relationship is supported, and whether its dimensions are measured. A real protein can appear in a hypothetical interaction. An established process can be drawn at an illustrative scale. A measured image can have an uncertain interpretation. Do not collapse these distinctions into a single “verified” flag.

A proposed relation should remain visually different from an established one. The house notation uses a dashed line for proposed and illustrative relations. It uses line and endpoint differences to distinguish association, activation, inhibition, transport, sequence and zoom. This is declared house notation, not a claim of formal SBGN compliance. SBGN defines distinct languages with their own semantics and specifications. [sbgn; sbgn-spec]

Captions should state meaningful omissions and the scope of inference. They should not contain production status, tests passed or assurances of quality. “Vasculature omitted” is a substantive limitation. “Passed QC” is not an explanation of the figure.

10. Accessibility is part of the explanation

Preserve meaning without colour alone. Use labels, shapes, line types or patterns to support distinctions. Check the most important relationships in grayscale and under appropriate colour-vision simulation. Avoid using a change in hue as the sole sign that a process is activated or inhibited. [wai-colour]

Every complete SVG has a title and a description. A useful long description explains the organisation and the intended takeaway, not merely a list of colours or the phrase “diagram of cells.” Complex figures can need a companion textual explanation. Patient materials require comprehension testing with their intended readers, not only a readability score. [wai-complex]

The default 7-point minimum at 180 mm width and 4.5:1 canvas-text contrast threshold are house settings. They are not substitutes for the current target journal's rules or the actual viewing conditions. The final production environment may substitute fonts; review the exported image there.

11. Keep observations and illustrations distinct

A realistic-looking generated image is not a microscope observation, a scan, a pathology specimen or patient evidence. Keep actual research images on a separate workflow with source preservation and permitted processing. The conceptual study-workflow specimen deliberately stops at the activity of microscopy; it does not manufacture a microscopy result.

Journal policies must be checked by figure category. Under the reviewed Elsevier policy, explanatory diagrams, data visualisations, primary research images and graphical abstracts have different conditions. Do not convert an exception for one class into blanket permission for another. The current target journal and any applicable disclosure requirement still control submission. [elsevier-ai]

Patient-related illustrations also need appropriate handling of consent, confidentiality and potentially identifying detail. Do not upload source photographs or clinical images to an external model merely because it would improve the drawing. Confidentiality and ethical conduct are part of the professional obligation. [ami-ethics]

12. Review the artifact that will actually be used

Scientific review should examine identity, topology, labels, omissions, evidence and interpretation. Visual review should examine reading order, hierarchy, clutter, legibility, line routing and colour/shape semantics. The reviewer must see the final image and its intended context, not only the prompt or a checklist.

Bind approval to the actual scene, used asset records, renderer and SVG. A later geometry edit, label change or substituted asset invalidates that binding. Keep the production record separate from the figure. Machine checks are useful because they make some errors cheap to catch; they are not a substitute for a qualified review of medical content.

The standard of success is the requested scientific communication, not completion of a checklist. When a defect is found, name it, change the relevant component and recheck the affected dependencies. Do not restart the entire figure or add unrelated tests unless a new material uncertainty requires it.

The executable visual grammar

A figure contains several kinds of statements

The scene language distinguishes biological identity, spatial assertions, mechanistic relations, drawing geometry, text and evidence. These distinctions prevent an agent from treating every object as an icon and every relation as an arrow.

A concise conceptual form is:

figure := commission + anatomical_frame + claims + compartments
          + entity_instances + structural_relations + visible_relations
          + labels + calibration + explanation

The executable representation is JSON governed by scene.schema.json. The renderer accepts version 1.0.0. Unknown fields are rejected. Extensions should be added deliberately to the schema, implementation and tests rather than silently placed into documents that the renderer ignores.

Commission and anatomical frame

Required top-level fields include id, title, description, audience, representation, frame, canvas, sources, claims, compartments, nodes, edges, labels and caption. subtitle, layout, relations and scale_bars are optional.

audience is one of scientific, clinical, student, patient or public. representation is schematic or anatomical. Anatomical mode rejects a bundled schematic asset used as primary anatomical geometry. It does not certify imported anatomical geometry.

The required frame fields are species, life_stage, view, laterality, projection and scale_policy. Laterality is left, right, bilateral or not-applicable; scale policy is not-to-scale or calibrated. Optional fields describe body region, sex characteristics, variants and clinical context. These strings make the assumptions explicit. Correct anatomical interpretation still requires expert review.

Canvas coordinates use a top-left origin with positive x to the right and positive y downward. Those are drawing coordinates, not anatomical superior/inferior or patient left/right. DICOM patient coordinates and image orientation require their own documented interpretation. [dicom]

Sources and claims

A source contains an ID, title, URL or documented provenance URN, and kind: reference, illustrative or original-data. A locator may identify a section or specific source feature. A claim contains ID, text, status and source IDs.

{
  "id": "claim-activation",
  "text": "The proposed regulator activates the response in this explanatory model.",
  "status": "proposed",
  "sources": ["source-study"]
}

Allowed statuses are established, proposed and illustrative. An established claim needs source records and cannot rely on a source marked merely illustrative. This is a traceability rule. It does not determine whether a paper supports the claim, whether it is reliable or whether a causal interpretation is justified.

Do not use a bibliography as a substitute for a claim map. Do not create a source record with a convincing title but no actual evidence. For unsourced examples, declare their illustrative status and do not give them the appearance of an established named pathway.

Entity instances and geometry

Each node instantiates an asset. The asset is the reusable drawing and its metadata; the node is its position and role in this figure. Repeated nodes may use the same asset ID, but node IDs must be unique.

{
  "id": "receptor",
  "asset": "receptor-single",
  "box": [433, 267, 140, 193],
  "compartment": "intracellular",
  "placement": "boundary"
}

A box is [x, y, width, height]. Asset aspect ratio is preserved inside the box. A tall box does not stretch a square asset into a tall cell. The drawing is centred within its box; some empty space can remain. This distinction is essential when making continuous tissue assemblies.

An alternative to a box is a named slot from the chosen layout. Exactly one of box or slot is required. The slot is a normalized positional scaffold. It does not supply content or determine a medically appropriate object size.

Optional rotation accepts 0, 90, 180 or 270 degrees. flip_x is accepted only for assets declared mirror-safe. Landmarks undergo the same rotation, mirroring and scale as the artwork. Primary anatomical assets must retain clinically meaningful laterality; a transformation that is geometrically supported is not automatically scientifically appropriate.

kind is entity or process. role is primary or support. A process node is required where consumption or production edges use the house reaction notation. A node note can document a substantive local limitation.

Compartments and boundaries

Compartments have an ID, label, box, fill, boundary style and kind. Their function is to establish the scene context rather than to act as generic decorative panels. Examples include extracellular and intracellular regions, cortex and medulla, or a defined tissue region.

For placement: inside, the node box must lie inside its declared rectangular compartment. For placement: boundary, it must overlap and extend across the boundary. unconstrained skips that geometric condition and should be used only for a stated reason.

These checks operate on rectangles. They do not prove containment within a cell's curved silhouette, registration to a 3D surface or correct membrane insertion. Do not interpret a successful rectangle test as anatomical proof. For a membrane-bound object, inspect the actual rendered membrane and the orientation of the extracellular and intracellular domains.

Structural relations

Structural relations express anatomical facts that generally should not become drawn arrows. Each record has an ID, subject node, predicate, object node and claim ID.

{
  "id": "nucleus-in-cell",
  "subject": "nucleus",
  "predicate": "part_of",
  "object": "cell",
  "claim": "cell-organisation"
}

The 25 predicates are:

Family Predicates
Part and enclosure part_of, contains, encloses, lines
Spatial relation adjacent_to, anterior_to, posterior_to, superior_to, inferior_to, medial_to, lateral_to, superficial_to, deep_to, proximal_to, distal_to
Continuity and route branches_from, drains_into, passes_through, continuous_with
Functional or attachment relation supplies, innervates, originates_from, inserts_into, contacts
Cross-view mapping corresponds_to

A structure enclosing another is not automatically its anatomical parent. A drawn overlap is not automatically contact. A vessel supplying an organ is not the same statement as that vessel being part of the organ. Keep the predicate specific.

The validator rejects self-relations, unresolved entities or claims, cycles in normalized proper part-whole relations, and direct opposite spatial assertions. It does not convert screen x/y positions into anatomical relations. Spatial truth must be checked in the declared anatomical frame. Different projections or instances should use appropriate correspondence records rather than contradictory positions assigned to the same object pair.

Visible relations

An edge has source and target references, a type, an ID and optional claim, polyline waypoints, text label and label position. The endpoints identify a node and a named landmark.

{
  "id": "activation",
  "source": {"node": "receptor", "landmark": "intracellular-domain"},
  "target": {"node": "effector", "landmark": "left"},
  "type": "activation",
  "claim": "claim-activation",
  "via": [[503, 574], [704, 574]],
  "label": "activates",
  "label_position": [553, 540]
}
Type Visual convention Scientific meaning
activation Open arrow end Positive influence in the stated model
inhibition T-bar end Negative influence in the stated model
association Plain undirected line Association or contact, not causation
transport Filled arrow end Directed movement of the stated material
consumption Filled arrow into a process node Input to the declared process
production Filled arrow out of a process node Output of the declared process
sequence Filled arrow end Order of activities or explanatory stages
zoom Dashed line without arrowhead Correspondence across illustrative scales

Biological edge types require a claim. Sequence and zoom do not imply biological causation. Proposed and illustrative claims are dashed regardless of arrow type. Where multiple relation types occur, include a concise explanatory legend or caption. An arrowhead shape cannot carry every scientific distinction by itself.

This is house notation. It is not an SBGN-ML exporter or a formal SBGN conformance implementation. Use an appropriate SBGN tool and the relevant Process Description, Activity Flow or Entity Relationship specification when formal interchange is required. [sbgn; sbgn-spec]

Labels and landmarks

A label has an ID, editable text and a box. Optional fields specify font size, alignment, role, target and leader waypoints. Alignment is left, right or centre; role is label, heading or note.

Landmarks are normalized points within an asset's view box. Their names should express the target, such as outer-membrane, nucleolus, urinary-pole or basement-membrane. The five generic landmarks supplied on every asset are useful for layout and non-anatomical connectors, but they are not substitutes for named anatomical targets. In imported assets they initially describe bounding-box positions.

The renderer wraps text using available font metrics, places editable text and draws a leader from an appropriate side of its box. It does not perform global automatic label-layout optimisation. It checks text-box overlap, but not every line crossing or obstruction by underlying anatomy. Explicit waypoints are supported when a leader needs to avoid another label or structure.

Final physical type size is calculated as:

font points = font coordinate size × print width in mm × 72
              / (canvas coordinate width × 25.4)

Do not shrink a complete figure after checking its labels without recalculating the effective size. The default profile uses 180 mm width with a 7-point minimum label size. These are configurable house values, not universal journal requirements.

Calibration and scale

A scale bar needs its drawing length, physical length, units per coordinate pixel, unit name and a source. The frame must be marked calibrated. The compiler checks that drawing length multiplied by calibration equals physical length. It does not establish whether the calibration source is valid or whether all elements share that scale.

Multi-scale scenes often cannot share one scale bar. Use separately calibrated views when data permit. Otherwise state that the scales are unequal and illustrative. A magnification label chosen for aesthetic balance is not calibration.

Assets, ontology and rights

The asset schema records drawing content, view box, landmarks, category, scale class, representation, viewpoint, mirroring policy, creator, licence, reference IDs, review state, aliases and limitations. Imported assets also record source URL. Optional ontology bindings have system, identifier, label, release and URL fields.

Ontology bindings must be looked up and confirmed. FIPAT, Uberon, Cell Ontology and MeSH support different purposes; their labels are not interchangeable identifiers. Record the version, especially where terminologies have changed. [fipat; ta2; uberon; cl; mesh]

reference_ids can include verification targets. schematic_unreviewed and imported_unreviewed must not be renamed to expert_reviewed because an AI inspected the file. A verified licence also does not imply verified biology.

Interchange and deliberate limits

SVG is the editable interchange format. PNG is a raster convenience format. PDF preserves the composed figure for viewing or publication workflows. The scene JSON preserves semantic and positional instructions. Editing a delivered SVG in another application does not automatically update the scene JSON; reconcile the edit before regenerating the figure.

No R, Blender, Figma, Illustrator, Inkscape, SBGN-ML, DICOM or molecular-coordinate round-trip adapter is claimed. Existing tools can read or supply appropriate files, but the tested local path is JSON to SVG and optional CairoSVG conversion. A broader interoperability claim requires actual round-trip tests in the target applications.

Anatomical judgment and art direction

The craft targets are distinct

“Netter-level” is a demanding reference to anatomical communication, not a colour palette. Regional anatomy needs correct relationships, informative exposure, convincing volume, selective detail and precise labels. “BioRender-level” usually invokes the clarity and consistency of scientific schematics, where abstraction, reusable objects, compartments and relation semantics are central. Neither target is reached by adding a familiar aesthetic to an unverified scene.

Use proprietary atlases and platforms only within their permissions. Their reputation is not permission to copy, trace or extract a competing library. Netter's image collection is licensed artwork, and BioRender's licences restrict standalone asset reuse. [netter-rights; biorender-rights]

The art direction below is a set of original working rules. It is not a claim to reproduce an artist's private technique, a licensed style pack or a substitute for anatomical training.

Nine useful visual languages

Regional anatomical plate

Use a dominant three-dimensional structure or coherent anatomical region. Establish the view, principal axis and adjacent landmarks. Let superficial structures partially occlude deeper ones unless the brief explicitly calls for a dissection or cutaway. Simplify irrelevant texture while preserving diagnostically or educationally important boundaries.

A plate should make shape and space comprehensible at its final size. Surface shading should support volume. Avoid a photographic level of indiscriminate detail, especially when it competes with a small named structure. Keep the lighting convention coherent across vessels, muscle, connective tissue and cut surfaces.

Clean sectional anatomy

Use a clearly declared cut plane and tissue order. Make the difference between a cut surface, a lumen and an external contour readable. The viewer should know whether the scene is a transverse section, longitudinal section, sagittal view or intentionally unfolded map without inferring it from colour alone.

A section is not a collection of adjacent organ icons. Registration and relative position must come from the source anatomy. The vessel-wall specimen is an explanatory concentric section; its wall thicknesses are not measurements. [openstax-vessels]

Tissue and histological schema

Prefer coherent tissue organisation over scattered cells. Cell polarity, neighbouring-cell interfaces and supporting layers should be visible. Select nuclear and cytoplasmic details that distinguish the structures relevant to the explanation. Illustrative cell counts and idealised geometry must not masquerade as a measured field.

A schematic may be informed by histology, but it is not a histology image. Do not add a stain-like texture simply to confer the appearance of experimental evidence. If an observed source is included elsewhere, distinguish the image and the explanatory overlay explicitly.

Mechanistic molecular schematic

Use consistent symbolic objects and typed relationships. Compartments should explain location. Receptor orientation and membrane placement should be intelligible. An association line, an activation arrow, an inhibition bar and material transport should remain distinct.

Treat a generic shape as a generic shape. A protein silhouette is not evidence of fold, binding surface, conformation or stoichiometry. Precise molecular assertions need a separate structure-based route. [sbgn; rcsb]

Evidence-based molecular landscape

Use structural and abundance information to decide what is present, which scale is represented and what is omitted. Keep uncertain or absent structural regions identifiable. Do not place arbitrarily detailed molecular shapes into a crowded background and call the result a realistic cell.

The useful lesson from the Goodsell gallery is the integration of evidence and deliberate omission. Its stated licence and individual acknowledgements must still be respected if an actual work is reused. [goodsell]

Surgical or interventional explanation

Keep the operative or imaging orientation stable across steps. Track which structures are intact, displaced, divided, exposed or removed. Each stage must be reviewed by an appropriate specialist. A plausible sequence of generated drawings must not become an invented operative protocol.

Show an instrument or device only in the supported anatomical relationship and intended use. Record the device version when it affects shape or placement. Do not treat a visually dramatic exposure as more informative than a clinically accurate one.

Patient explanation

Reduce conceptual burden without making the anatomy false. Choose ordinary language where it preserves meaning, limited visual detail and a clear purpose. Respect the reader's dignity, privacy and context. Include only the degree of graphic detail necessary for the explanation.

Comprehension testing matters. A pleasing illustration can still mislead a reader about relative risk, treatment effects or what is happening in their own body. Do not use this route to produce unreviewed patient-specific medical advice.

Editorial graphical abstract

Let the supported finding dominate. Use methods and context only insofar as they are needed to understand that finding. Distinguish the actual result from a proposed mechanism or implication. Do not invent a chart, number or effect direction for compositional symmetry.

Publisher policy is a design constraint at the start, not a final export setting. A permitted explanatory diagram does not imply that the same generative process is allowed for a graphical abstract. [elsevier-ai]

Descriptive research workflow

Show material, activity, observation and information as different things. Sequence arrows explain order, not biological causation. A sample should not visually become data until a real measurement is represented. Omit operating details that do not serve the reader's question.

The workflow specimen uses equipment symbols and editorial spacing. It contains no assay output, diagnostic image, patient identifier or instructions for conducting an experiment.

Spatial invariants before pictorial refinement

Identity and correspondence

A structure must retain identity across views, scales and states. Match named landmarks rather than relying on a similar silhouette. A cross-sectional inset needs a justified relationship to the whole-region locator. A magnified molecular symbol needs an identified parent region rather than a floating circular inset.

For repeated views, keep source versions and relevant labels consistent. Do not compare a detailed reference-derived structure with a generic stock icon and imply that the difference is biological.

Continuity and branching

Draw connected lumens continuously. A crossing line is not an anastomosis. A branch needs a documented origin and course. A nerve ending or vessel segment should not vanish merely because a label occupies the remaining space.

When a system is unfolded for clarity, preserve its connection order and disclose the geometric transformation. The nephron specimen prioritises tubular continuity and separates associated collecting drainage from the nephron itself. It deliberately omits vasculature and filtration ultrastructure. [openstax-kidney]

Orientation and laterality

Lock anatomical orientation before placement. Do not horizontally flip a heart, organ, limb, image plane or surgical view to improve balance. For a radiological or sectional display, distinguish image display convention from the subject's anatomy and coordinates. [dicom]

Text such as “left” must refer to the declared subject frame, not merely the left side of the page. A bilaterally displayed body is not an exemption from laterality checks on its component structures.

Layers, cut edges and occlusion

Use a stable visual treatment for intact surfaces, cut surfaces, deep structures and annotations. A thick contour can identify the outer envelope; a finer line can identify internal structure. A section edge may need a distinct texture or colour, but it should not resemble a fibrous capsule unless one is actually being depicted.

Opacity is not a universal solution to hidden anatomy. A transparent overlay can make a structure appear absent, multiply overlapping edges or destroy depth interpretation. When using a ghosted layer, explain which tissue remains present and why it is shown faintly. Sometimes a second registered panel is more truthful and clearer.

Scale and proportion

Distinguish proportional drawing from calibrated measurement. A schematic can enlarge a receptor or a junction for explanation, but it must not imply that the enlargement is anatomically proportional. Separate scales explicitly. Preserve one scale in a matched comparison unless the comparison itself concerns scale.

Do not infer developmental anatomy by shrinking an adult asset. Do not infer a species from a generic silhouette. The frame and asset limitations should prevent those substitutions from passing unnoticed.

Construct a coherent material language

Colour and texture should help the reader recognise tissue classes or follow a defined variable. Do not make every material glossy. Blood vessels, bone, cartilage, muscle, nerve and connective tissue do not need the same edge softness or highlight. A small number of consistent cues is often more legible than many individually realistic textures.

Use the light source to reveal volume, not to create drama. Keep cast shadows separate from diagram semantics. In a schematic, eliminate a shadow when it implies three-dimensional distance that the model does not intend. In an anatomical plate, preserve subtle form transitions when they are necessary to identify structure.

A useful line hierarchy distinguishes silhouette, section boundary, internal feature and annotation. As a house starting point, the dominant contour can be approximately twice the annotation line weight, with internal features between them. These ratios are design defaults, not medical or publication standards. Adjust them to the actual figure size, complexity and output device.

Use saturation and contrast deliberately. The target structure may deserve stronger colour than context, but a faint context structure should not be mistaken for a missing or diseased structure. Red and blue must not be used as an unqualified universal equation of artery and vein with oxygenation. Explain the intended colour convention. [openstax-vessels]

Reference-guided anatomical production sequence

Begin with a written region and structure list. Assemble the relevant, lawfully accessible references and record what each supports. Establish the frame, projection and intended cut or exposure. Build a sparse structural drawing or inspect the selected 3D geometry. Confirm the principal relationships before refining outlines.

Next, define label landmarks on the approved geometry. Set the composition at the intended dimensions, reserving space for labels and any necessary locator. Resolve occlusion, depth order and local magnification. Apply material cues and selective detail only after those relationships are stable.

Render without labels once to inspect anatomy, then with labels to inspect the explanation. A specialist reviews the complete figure against its sources; a visual reviewer examines legibility and interpretation. Retain the editable geometry. Do not flatten labels into a generative image and then attempt to recover their accuracy through OCR.

For a generative painting stage, supply the approved geometry as a constraint or control reference rather than merely a prose subject. Protect the silhouette, structure count, connections, cut plane and landmark locations. Reject a result that changes them even if the new rendering looks more impressive. Add typography through deterministic composition after image review. The present Python package supplies specifications and vector composition; it does not implement or authenticate an external generative painting service.

For each acquired asset, store source, rights, version, view, scale class, anatomical identity, named landmarks, important omissions and a genuine expert review record. Use reviewed component families rather than a large undifferentiated dump. Add multiple views or states only when each has a documented scientific purpose. This is how coverage grows without multiplying uncertainty.

Representation and dignity

Declare which variation is relevant to the commission. Do not make one demographic group the unlabelled default “normal” and another group a variation unless the scientific context genuinely requires a defined reference. Skin colour should not be used as an unsupported marker of disease, tissue oxygenation or biological difference.

Patient-identifying source material requires appropriate authority and careful handling. Respectful representation is not satisfied by adding diverse-looking silhouettes to an otherwise inaccurate figure. The scientific detail and clarity should be comparable across represented groups. Professional ethics include honesty, permissions and confidentiality. [ami-ethics]

Frequent failures and the appropriate repair

When a figure contains too much detail, remove detail unrelated to its explanatory question rather than shrinking everything. When anatomy looks like disconnected icons, return to the regional or tissue assembly and reconstruct the relationships. When labels cross, move the label space or change a specific route, not the identity or orientation of the organ.

When a process arrow is ambiguous, name the relation and select the correct endpoint convention. When a source does not support a claim, weaken or remove the claim; do not compensate with a more authoritative visual style. When a generated rendering changes a required structure, return to the approved geometry instead of treating the change as artistic improvement.

When a figure looks polished but cannot answer its scientific question, the problem is the explanation. Additional shading, texture and colour will not solve it.

Agent workflows and handoffs

A small number of explicit responsibilities

The commissioning role defines the reader, question, scientific scope and deliverable. The research role verifies the relevant sources and rights. The scientific modeller expresses entities, compartments, structural relations and claim status. The illustrator or renderer creates the geometry and composition. The scientific reviewer assesses medical truth; the visual reviewer assesses communication and appearance.

These responsibilities need not correspond to six separate agents. Creating more agents does not create independent medical expertise. A single agent can coordinate the mechanical work, but it cannot manufacture a qualified human approval by assigning a second AI the role name “anatomist.” Keep that distinction explicit.

A handoff should carry the actual scene, assets, supporting sources, outstanding material uncertainties and the requested next transformation. Do not hand off only a prose promise of what was supposedly completed. Downstream work must be based on the current artifact rather than a stale summary of it.

Workflow A: a scientific schematic

Translate the commission into a statement such as “Explain a membrane interaction while distinguishing physical association from intracellular regulation.” Select a compartment-oriented layout. Identify entities and their required side of the membrane. Write claims for each biological relation and mark the status of each.

Search the existing library for suitable symbolic objects. Inspect their limitations and landmarks. If an asset is sufficient, reuse it. If its identity is wrong or its geometry cannot express the required feature, acquire or create an appropriate asset rather than relabelling it. Do not call a generic single-pass receptor a known multi-pass protein.

Write the scene JSON. Use validate, address actual errors, then render an SVG and separate record. Inspect the result at the intended width. Confirm that labels touch the intended features and that compartment assignments agree with the visible geometry. Add the meaningful caption and required source attribution. Qualified review precedes authoritative scientific or medical use.

The completed signalling example is a syntax and composition model, not evidence for a specific pathway. Its hypothetical status is intentional. Agents must not replace hypothetical names with real proteins while retaining the same unsourced arrows.

Workflow B: a reference-based anatomical plate

Specify the region, exact view, laterality, life stage and required structures. Select a lawful reference set and an appropriate geometry route. The source may be an approved vector illustration, a reviewed model or an illustrator's reconstruction. Reference images are not automatically a redistributable asset library.

Resolve anatomical relationships before stylistic refinement. Use the structural relation contract to express the important adjacency, continuity, hierarchy and orientation assertions. Verify any ontology bindings separately. Import the geometry only when it is in the supported passive SVG subset or compose the final vector exported from the external anatomical workflow.

The imported asset initially remains unreviewed. Define named landmarks using actual structures, not only the asset rectangle. Preserve the original aspect ratio. Do not reflect an organ to fill a page. Use anatomical mode for primary anatomical geometry, and treat supporting schematic glyphs explicitly as support material.

The human scientific reviewer should see the source references, the exact final view and the named required structures. The visual reviewer should inspect the image at the intended print or screen dimensions. Source or geometry changes after approval require review of the affected content and renewed artifact binding.

Workflow C: evidence synthesis or a manuscript mechanism

Start from the manuscript's supported claims and uncertainty, not the visual logic of a familiar pathway. Distinguish established relations, proposed mechanisms, associations and study activities. Source every biological edge at the claim level. Where the evidence is indirect, say so in the claim and use the appropriate visual distinction.

Avoid including every plausible biological intermediate. An intermediate that has not been justified can make the figure appear mechanistically complete while weakening its truth. Use a bounded unknown region, a proposed relation or a separate explanation when the evidence cannot establish the missing connection.

Treat causal interpretation as a scientific assertion requiring its own support. The compositor does not turn association into causation. It enforces the declared notation and traceability, while the researcher and reviewer determine whether the interpretation is warranted.

Workflow D: multi-scale explanation

Identify the parent region before drawing an inset. State which feature is magnified and what information the magnification adds. Use a zoom relation without an arrowhead. Keep orientation correspondence where it matters; explain an intentional reorientation.

Do not use one scale bar across views with unequal magnification. If scales are not calibrated, make that explicit. Keep proportional anatomy and symbolic molecular detail conceptually separate. The multi-scale tissue example uses three uncalibrated explanatory scales rather than fabricated magnification values.

Workflow E: publication-specific delivery

Classify the figure before choosing a generative route. An illustrative schematic, graphical abstract, primary image and data visualisation can face different restrictions. Check the current target journal's official rules, then choose a compatible process and required disclosure. Do not claim acceptance because a generic exporter can produce a PDF.

Use the source SVG and scene as the editable master. Export the actual requested size and format. Inspect the final output for typography, clipping, colour and changed line appearance. Publication mode requires review records bound to the current artifact, but cannot verify the reviewers' identities or the publisher's decision.

When the journal requests AI disclosure, a methodological explanation or an attribution, include it in the appropriate substantive location. Keep production activity and quality-check narration separate. These are different kinds of information.

Producing an external image-generation brief

python -m medillustrate brief figure.scene.json -o figure.production.md compiles the scientific contract, composition constraints, assets, structural relations and claim ledger. This is an external production brief, not a call to an image model.

A useful anatomical brief names the approved geometry, required structures, view, cut surfaces, lighting, material cues, omissions and invariants. It explicitly forbids adding structures to improve realism. A useful schematic brief identifies compartments, abstraction level, line semantics, evidence status, relative scale and text that must remain editable.

A generative model output is a candidate rendering. It is not a reviewer, a source, a clinical observation or evidence that an anatomical statement is true. Do not allow attractive output to overwrite approved topology. Keep deterministic labels and arrows separate until the image itself has been reviewed.

Prompt patterns

Anatomy commission pattern

Create a regional anatomical explanation of [region] for [reader]. Use [approved reference/geometry], in [documented view and laterality]. The required structures are [list]; the required relationships are [specific relations]. Preserve [named landmarks, continuity and cut plane]. Omit [declared exclusions]. Mechanism commission pattern

Explain [process] within [compartments]. The established claims are [claim IDs and sources]. The proposed claims are [IDs and limitations]. Distinguish association, material movement, activation and inhibition. Use [specified abstraction level] and disclose unequal scales. Select or create assets that actually represent the required classes. Do not invent intermediate steps, counts or measured results. Keep the composition focused on [single explanatory sentence].

Correction pattern

Preserve the approved figure except for [named defect]. Change [specific node, relation, landmark or label]. Retain [explicit invariants]. Re-render and recheck [affected dependencies]. Do not restyle unrelated content, change the evidence status or regenerate the entire figure without a material reason.

These patterns are deliberately affirmative about the requested work. Prohibitions specify scientific failure boundaries; they should not become the entire creative brief.

Control records without clutter

The control record may contain sources consulted, source checks, alternative assets, decisions, issues, reviewer statements and artifact hashes. It is established alongside the work and maintained separately. The complete figure should contain only the scientific explanation, necessary attribution or disclosure, and meaningful limitations.

Do not insert approval statements, tool logs, internal file paths or check counts into the SVG description or figure caption. The SVG description is an accessibility explanation of the figure. Its purpose is not to document how the agent worked.

An organisation can store approved records in a controlled system and validate reviewer identity externally. The present record is plain JSON. Its hashes detect a mismatch with the current figure; they are not a digital signature, credential service or tamper-proof audit system.

Completion and revision

Completion means that the requested figure and its editable source exist, answer the scientific question and have the required review or a clearly stated missing-review limitation. Do not call a layout scaffold a completed plate. Do not call a provider directory an installed asset library. Do not call an executable benchmark protocol a result.

For a new defect, state the requirement it violates and make the corresponding change. Reuse valid prior checks for unchanged content. Work should converge on the requested artifact, not on an expanding collection of checklists and reports.

Research and open resources

Scope and selection

This is a focused primary-source review for building a practical medical illustration toolkit, checked on 5 October 2026. It is not a systematic census of every anatomical atlas, artist, publication standard or open-source application. The source directory distinguishes reviewed material, discovery-only records, incomplete retrieval and recommended verification targets. It does not claim that every listed source has supplied an independently verified asset.

The review covers professional medical illustration, proprietary reference boundaries, open scientific artwork, terminology, anatomical and molecular reference data, diagram notation, accessibility, publication policy and vector export. Sources were chosen for the decisions they support rather than to maximise the length of a bibliography. The resulting rules distinguish published guidance from original house defaults.

The major design decision

Separate anatomical reconstruction from explanatory schematics. Anatomical plates need reference-supported spatial truth and expert judgment. Mechanistic diagrams need explicit abstraction and relationship semantics. A general image prompt is not a substitute for either. AMI's description of the profession supports treating scientific understanding and visual communication as linked responsibilities. [ami-profession]

The toolkit consequently supplies two routes: deterministic schematic composition using original or licensed vectors, and composition of reference-supported imported anatomical geometry. It does not claim to generate a clinically reliable body from text. A separate structure-based route is required when measured molecular geometry matters. [rcsb]

Terminology and semantic resources

FIPAT provides anatomical terminology resources, while the TA2 viewer supplies a particular versioned terminology interface. The FIPAT portal currently lists Terminologia Anatomica Humana 2024 among its resources. Record a named terminology and edition rather than assuming every anatomy label belongs to the same version. Uberon supports anatomical ontology work across species. Cell Ontology supports natural cell-type identity. MeSH supports indexing and discovery. None supplies a guarantee that a drawn shape, anatomical course or label endpoint is correct. An anatomical relation contract should remain distinct from display geometry. A part_of assertion, an anterior/posterior relation and a screen position express different things. Similarly, DICOM's patient and image-coordinate definitions must not be replaced with an illustrator's intuitive left/right convention. [dicom]

Notation and existing tools

SBGN provides distinct Process Description, Activity Flow and Entity Relationship languages, with formal specifications and supporting software. The present toolkit's visible edge grammar is declared house notation. It does not export SBGN-ML or claim conformance merely because some arrowheads are familiar. Use a formal editor and validator when that exchange requirement matters. [sbgn; sbgn-spec]

Bioicons already supports vector-editor workflows, and diagrams.net documents use of biochemical clipart. Reuse those existing capabilities where they fit the task. The new code here concentrates on the specific scene specification, traceability, asset metadata, local composition and checks rather than rebuilding a general graphics editor. [bioicons; drawio-bioicons]

CairoSVG provides optional PNG and PDF conversion. It is not an anatomical renderer. Conversion fidelity, physical size and font behaviour still require inspection in the production environment. [cairosvg]

Publication policy findings

The reviewed Elsevier policy distinguishes explanatory diagrams, data visualisations, primary research images and graphical abstracts. It permits some AI-assisted explanatory work with oversight and disclosure, while applying different restrictions to primary imagery and graphical abstracts. In particular, its graphical-abstract guidance does not allow general-purpose AI image generators. Check the exact journal and current policy before choosing a production method. [elsevier-ai]

The full current Nature Portfolio AI policy could not be retrieved in this review. The package therefore supplies a recheck requirement rather than repeating an older policy from memory or asserting universal permission. PLOS figure guidance is included as a technical preparation reference, not a guarantee that the generic house profile meets every current journal requirement. [nature-ai; plos-figures]

No journal acceptance certification is claimed. Figure type, integrity, disclosure, source rights, physical dimensions, export format and editorial requirements all remain part of the actual submission decision.

Textbook references and licensing caution

The explanatory tissue and vessel examples use anatomical facts as references and original drawing geometry. No textbook illustration has been copied into the library. Current OpenStax page licensing must be checked for the actual item and version; this distribution does not assume that every page or edition is uniformly CC BY 4.0. Some organelle, cell and connective-tissue records are marked as verification targets rather than completed source checks. [openstax-epithelium; openstax-vessels]

The nephron example follows an unfolded conceptual route and states its omissions. The signalling specimen does not claim to represent an experimentally established named mechanism. The distinction between original schematic geometry and copied reference artwork should remain visible in future asset acquisition. [openstax-kidney; openstax-signalling]

What is potentially distinctive, and what is not established

The proposed integration combines a machine-readable biological scene, separate structural and visible relations, source-linked claims, named anatomical landmarks, explicit abstraction limits, local vectors, guarded imports and version-bound human review records. The package implements these components within its stated limits.

That integration is not proof of a novel market category or of superiority to existing tools. No exhaustive feature census, expert-rated visual comparison or independent clinical validation has been completed. The comparative protocol explains how to test quality fairly, including open collections paired with established editors. A defensible claim should name the task set, versions, reviewers, evaluation criteria and uncertainty rather than use an unqualified “no equal.”

Comparative evaluation: protocol, not results

What would justify a quality claim?

A claim of Netter-level anatomical communication requires much more than attractive example images. It needs expert assessment of spatial anatomy, labels, informative exposure, completeness for the brief and visual explanation. A claim of BioRender-level schematic capability needs assessment of coherent scientific scenes, relation semantics, asset adequacy, editing, export and reliability. A claim of market superiority requires a defined comparison set and a study, not a feature list.

No such evaluation has been completed for this package. The provided 24 task briefs are a reusable benchmark specification. The eight specimens are demonstrations, not blinded evaluation outputs. No performance scores or human approvals are supplied.

Compare complete workflows fairly

Compare tools at the level at which people actually use them. An icon collection should not be treated as a failed illustration application because it does not draw arrows or place labels. A general vector editor paired with an open asset collection is a legitimate workflow comparator. An anatomical 3D source paired with an illustrator's vector or painting workflow is another.

A suitable comparison set could include Bioicons plus an SVG editor, Servier artwork plus an editor, NIH BioArt Source plus an editor, Z-Anatomy plus a compatible 3D/2D workflow, a formal SBGN editor for pathways, and this toolkit with the same permitted source access. Include licensed commercial workflows only where permission allows the evaluation and use of resulting figures. Document tool versions, asset licences, operator experience and model/backend settings. [bioicons; servier; nih-bioart; zanatomy; sbgn]

Do not penalise a comparator for refusing to fabricate research data, missing copyrighted artwork or lack of access to the same references. Conversely, do not quietly give one system an expert-produced finished anatomical drawing while asking another to infer it from text.

Tasks and reference answers

The task file covers anatomy, tissue sections, tubular continuity, membranes, cellular organisation, matched views, workflows, multi-scale scenes, cutaways, exploded layers, section registration, procedure explanation, innervation, vascular routes, topography, histology schemata, molecular binding, reaction networks, feedback, cell interfaces, development, pathology and device context.

For each task, prepare a fixed brief, source package, required structures or relations, important omissions, permitted assets, final dimensions and reference answer. An independent specialist should approve the reference answer and the critical-error list. Do not use the model's own output as the reference against which its correctness is scored.

The 24 supplied briefs are generic. They become an evaluation dataset only after those task-specific materials have been supplied. Keep some tasks and references out of development so that repeated tuning does not turn the test into a memory exercise.

Scoring

Rate each domain from 0 to 4 using explicit definitions: 0 is unusable or materially wrong; 1 has major failures; 2 is usable only after substantial correction; 3 meets the defined requirement with limited correction; 4 meets it well without a material identified defect.

Domain Weight Evaluation question
Scientific accuracy 30 Are structures, claims, labels and evidence interpretations correct?
Spatial topology 20 Are continuity, adjacency, laterality, layers and orientation correct?
Communication 20 Does the intended reader understand the intended distinction?
Visual craft 15 Does the composition use form, hierarchy and detail effectively?
Accessibility 5 Does meaning survive the required viewing and accessibility conditions?
Editability and reproducibility 5 Can the result be edited and regenerated without losing its meaning?
Licensing and traceability 5 Are rights, sources and adaptations appropriately recorded?

The total is the sum of weight multiplied by domain rating, divided by four. It ranges from 0 to 100. A critical scientific or integrity error fails the task irrespective of the total. An aesthetically excellent figure with wrong laterality must not pass because its other scores are high.

Critical errors

Predefine critical errors for each task. Examples include wrong anatomical identity or laterality, impossible connections, a label identifying the wrong structure, unsupported causal interpretation, a fabricated primary research image, a materially false scale, an invented result or impermissible redistribution of proprietary artwork.

Define borderline errors in advance as well. A deliberate omission disclosed in the brief is not the same as a missing required structure. A schematic enlargement declared as not to scale is not the same as a false quantitative scale. These distinctions need consistent reviewer guidance.

Human evaluation

Use at least two independent qualified reviewers for scientific judgments and appropriate visual reviewers for craft and communication. Randomise output order and remove tool branding where lawful and feasible. Do not blind reviewers to scientific context needed for a valid judgment. Record individual ratings before adjudicating disagreements.

Report task-level scores, critical failures, disagreements, uncertainty and time to a usable reviewed figure. Account for the paired design because the tools receive the same tasks. A bootstrap across tasks can describe uncertainty in paired performance differences, provided its assumptions and limitations are clear. Do not turn a small task set with wide uncertainty into a universal claim of superiority.

Record time spent on source preparation, initial generation, corrections and human review separately. A system that generates quickly but requires extensive anatomical correction has not necessarily improved the production workflow.

Medical Illustration Studio

An agent-readable medical illustration skill, visual grammar and local Python toolkit. It turns a scientific scene specification into editable SVG and, with the optional exporter, PNG or PDF. It also provides original schematic assets, a layout atlas, anatomical art direction and a reference directory.

Open atlas.html for the offline illustrated handbook, searchable assets and eight example figures. The HTML contains its own artwork and does not require a server or network connection.

What is implemented

The package includes 77 original vector records: 75 schematic assets, one continuous epithelial tissue assembly and one process glyph. They cover cells, organelles, membranes, molecular symbols, blood cells, tissue motifs and selected laboratory equipment. These are distinct drawings or structural representations, not colour variants counted as separate biological objects.

The compiler supports explicit positions or one of 28 positional layout scaffolds, aspect-preserving placement, quarter-turn rotation, controlled mirroring, named label landmarks, editable text, compartment backgrounds, eight connector types, 25 structural-relation predicates, evidence-linked claims and calibrated scale bars. Structural relations are not automatically converted into visual arrows.

It includes strict scene and asset schemas, local SVG import, bounded raster/PDF export, separate provenance records, review binding, a human-rating benchmark scorer and executable tests. There is no network dependency in rendering and no requirement for a model API key. An AI agent researches and writes the scene; the deterministic compositor builds it.

What is not claimed

This is not a trained anatomical image model, a full human atlas, a molecular modelling package, a 3D anatomy reconstruction service or a replacement for a qualified medical illustrator. The bundled assets and specimens have not received independent medical review. No blinded Netter/BioRender comparison or market-wide competitive evaluation has been performed.

Anatomical plate production uses reference-supported imported geometry and external expert review. The renderer can preserve and compose that geometry; it cannot infer that a branch, tissue interface, lesion or label is medically correct. A structurally valid scene is not automatically a scientifically true figure.

The source directory includes verified pages, discovery-only records and verification targets. Its status fields distinguish them. External collections are not included in the asset count and their download integrations are not implemented.

Use with an agent

Keep the package together so that SKILL.md, the module, examples and reference documents remain accessible. Configure the agent to read SKILL.md when creating or revising a medical illustration. No agent configuration or repository has been modified by this distribution.

Start with the actual scientific question, not a request for an attractive picture. For example:

Explain the apical-to-basal organisation of an epithelial barrier for postgraduate readers. Preserve a continuous epithelial layer, identifiable junctions and the basement membrane. Use a sectional schematic, not a realistic histology image. Keep all labels editable. Do not invent disease changes or measured dimensions. Deliver SVG, scene JSON and a separate source/review record.

An anatomical commission should instead specify the exact region, reference geometry, required structures, view, laterality and qualified reviewer. A mechanism commission should specify evidence for each relation and distinguish observed, inferred and hypothetical content.

Import rather than redraw unnecessarily

Import a locally downloaded, appropriately licensed SVG with import-svg, then pass the resulting asset JSON to --library. A catalogue object with an assets array is also accepted. Existing bundled IDs cannot be silently replaced.

The accepted SVG subset supports passive vector geometry, groups, text, inline presentation styles, local gradients and clipping paths. It intentionally excludes scripts, external images, use, filters, embedded web content, CSS blocks and external resources. Export unsupported artwork as plain SVG paths in a trusted editor, retain its rights information and review the converted geometry. The importer is not a general lossless converter for every SVG application.

Review-bound export

render --record writes a separate control record with hashes for the resolved scene, used assets, renderer source and generated SVG. This record deliberately contains no approvals. Scientific and visual reviewers supply their own identities, qualifications, approval statements and dates after reviewing the actual artifact. Publication mode checks the supplied record and rejects stale bindings.

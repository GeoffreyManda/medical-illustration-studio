## View-specific constraints

**Kidney, opened section:** keep the distinction between the exterior and the exposed section. Show continuity through the illustrated collecting passages. Do not imply that the small selection of drawn vessels represents the complete renal circulation. The schematic section does not establish a measured number of pyramids or a patient-specific appearance.

**Kidney, external surface:** internal cortex, medulla and calyces are not visible target locations. The selected hilar structures and external landmarks do not amount to a full topographic dissection. Laterality must not be inferred merely from the direction in which the asset happens to be placed.

**Arterial wall:** the concentric organisation is a deliberate cutaway. The representative coats are not measurements of a particular artery's wall thickness or lumen diameter. The art does not diagnose vascular disease.

**Peripheral nerve:** a labelled group of fascicles is not a specimen-specific map of functional innervation. The decorative count and spacing of fibres should not be read quantitatively.

## Referencing a new anatomical plate

Record the source, view and relationship it supports; the exact figure or page when relevant; any adaptation rights; and the local geometry or landmark affected. Distinguish a source author's anatomical statement from a project author's compositional choice. Keep an uncertain or omitted structure explicit. An agent must not claim that a source supports a finer level of anatomical detail than it actually provides.

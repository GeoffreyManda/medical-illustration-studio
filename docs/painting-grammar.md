# Anatomical painting grammar

## The drawing must work before the paint

An anatomical plate is an explanation of a view. Start with the structures and spatial relationships needed for that explanation. Establish a silhouette, local landmarks, visible overlaps, opened compartments and connected structures before selecting a material palette. A glossy surface cannot repair a wrong insertion, an interrupted lumen or an inconsistent section plane.

Use five separately inspectable descriptions:

1. **Anatomy:** named structures, source-linked claims, viewpoint, section plane, laterality and omitted context.
2. **Geometry:** contours, visible boundaries, continuity, overlaps and leader targets.
3. **Values:** large light-dark masses that make depth and cut surfaces intelligible.
4. **Materials:** tissue-specific colour variation, fibres, translucency and surface detail.
5. **Lettering:** editable labels, leaders, title, explanation and scale conventions.

In the current toolkit, geometry, texture groups and lettering are editable SVG. A provider-generated painting is an additional raster appearance layer. It does not replace the anatomical description or manufacture a set of editable tissue structures.

## Shape and spatial treatment

An organ should have a specific contour rather than a generic rounded symbol. Local curvature changes at a hilum, pole, cut edge or attachment should remain visible. Preserve anatomical asymmetry when it is part of the selected view; do not mirror an organ merely to fit a label bank.

Select a consistent light direction. Model broad planes first, then curved transitions, then the small shadows that describe real overlaps. Keep shadows subordinate to the explanatory drawing. A dark decorative border around every structure flattens the dissection and makes delicate tissue boundaries look like diagram icons.

A cut surface needs a visible relationship to the remaining volume. Its rim, exposed substance and opened interior should not share one undifferentiated fill. An opened vessel is not a solid cylinder with a coloured disk pasted on the end. A tissue section is not a stack of unrelated blocks unless the educational purpose explicitly calls for abstraction.

Use edge softness selectively. A genuine occlusion or cut edge can be precise; a rounded surface turning away from the light can soften. Avoid equally sharp detail everywhere. Context may be quieter than the focal structure, but required neighbouring relationships must remain readable.

## Material vocabulary

These are art-direction conventions for original educational drawings, not measurements of tissue optics or substitutes for specimen-specific references.

| Material | Useful visual treatment | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Capsule or thin connective envelope | Restrained highlight following curvature; thin visible boundary | An opaque white shell that hides the organ contour |
| Organ parenchyma | Broad tonal masses and low-amplitude local variation | Plastic gradients, heavy noise or uniform repeated dots |
| Directional tissue | Marks that follow the local anatomical direction and taper into the form | A global diagonal hatch unrelated to structure |
| Adipose tissue | Unequal soft lobules, gentle occlusion and restrained warm colour | A regular grid of identical yellow circles |
| Opened lumen | Clear wall/interior distinction and continuous visible passage | A solid tube, blocked branch or unexplained opening |
| Vessel wall | Distinct but integrated coats around a coherent lumen | Floating concentric rings with no material continuity |
| Peripheral nerve | Fascicles as groups within their surrounding envelopes | Random cables mistaken for a labelled fascicular section |
| Background context | Reduced contrast and detail while retaining spatial cues | Blur that erases an important anatomical relationship |

Texture must explain a material or organisation. It should not be added merely because an asset looks too simple. Prefer fewer well-directed marks over dense procedural decoration. Avoid identical spacing and repeated lobules where they imply a regularity not supported by the drawing brief.

## A plate rather than an icon sheet

Give the primary anatomy enough area to be studied. A full plate usually needs a dominant view, not a dashboard of similarly sized miniatures. Add a secondary view only when it resolves orientation or shows a different necessary relationship. Keep its scale and viewpoint explicit. A magnified inset needs a defined parent location and an honest scale convention.

Place labels in stable banks when that reduces crossings. Let target height influence ordering; route a leader to the intended tissue rather than simply to the closest contour. A label can move without its target moving. A target correction is an anatomical decision, not a typographic adjustment.

Avoid painting text into the artwork. Keep names, arrows and lettering native through revisions and export. Do not add invented signatures, credentials, atlas branding, slogans or institutional marks. The previously generated multi-panel composites are not bundled as anatomical exemplars: visual polish cannot excuse mislabeled or wrongly connected structures.

## From an anatomical source to a candidate painting

Use an identified, legally usable source to establish geometry. Retain explicit fields for what is visible, what is omitted and what must not change. Generate the unlabelled art layer only after the reference drawing is interpretable at contour and value stages.

A useful refinement instruction names the defect and desired treatment: “Reduce the broad specular band on the capsule; preserve the pole contours and hilum; introduce a softer turn into shadow.” An instruction such as “make it medical and premium” leaves anatomy and appearance underspecified.

For a regional repaint, specify the tissue region and the structures that remain invariant. The studio can preserve outside-mask pixels exactly after generation. Inside the mask, a model may still alter anatomy. A candidate is not ready merely because the rendering looks more natural.

Compare the source and candidate at the same framing, then inspect the final plate at intended print size. If a model changes topology, reject that candidate or correct it against the reference. Do not keep it because its brushwork is attractive. If a label becomes ambiguous, repair its target before adoption. Human landmark confirmation remains different from independent medical review.

## Visual standard and present scope

The target is the explanatory strength of a carefully drawn anatomical atlas: recognisable structures, coherent volume, selective detail, restrained lettering and a purposeful viewpoint. The delivered four anatomical studies improve substantially on the original glyph vocabulary, but remain idealised vector illustrations. They are not presented as a reviewed regional human atlas or as demonstrated Netter-equivalent paintings.

The agent's job is to reduce the gap in the drawing itself. More checklists, model calls, or material names do not compensate for an unresolved anatomical form.
